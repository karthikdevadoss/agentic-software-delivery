## What this audit did and did not touch

**Did:** 63 real questions to the production Standing Interview via the API plus 2 more through a real browser (**65 of the 80-request cap**, cap enforced in the probe's own code); a real-Chromium pass over the production page; a full read of the pipeline source; a run of the hermetic SI suite; deterministic re-scoring of every live answer against the shipped gates and against this audit's own oracle; registration of 43 backlog items.

# Executive summary — the ten gaps, in plain English

1. **The system is doing what it was told; it was told the wrong thing.** The Owner's complaint is that answers volunteer what he did *not* do. The prompt contains a section headed *"WHAT YOU DID, AND WHERE THAT STOPPED"* that instructs the model to state the work and then *"the boundary of it in one sentence"*, and to *"pick the single thing an interviewer is most likely to ask next (exact names, exact settings)"*. The "I don't have the topic names, partition counts, consumer-group design…" sentence is not the model disobeying. It is the model complying. This is a **specification failure, not a model failure**.

2. **The "not used" facts are handed to the model as speakable content.** For *"explain your experience with Kafka"* the pipeline composes and sends the line *"Your own record says: hands-on at BCBSA; not used at NRG Energy… an absence is at most one closing sentence."* The absence is in the context, and permission to say it is in the same sentence. "We didn't use Kafka at NRG" is the specified output.

3. **Every gate passes every answer the Owner rejected.** This was measured, not argued: the three production Kafka answers and the three production Marsh answers were run back through the shipped leak scan, voice gate, neighbour check and the acceptance gate's own `_check_generic_kafka`. All four returned empty for all six answers. A sprint can be 117/117 green, 15/15 on its acceptance gate, and still ship the thing the Owner is unhappy about, because nothing in the suite is looking at it.

4. **The one counter that was supposed to cap this cannot see most of it.** The voice gate counts "limitation sentences" and allows two. It does not recognise *"I don't remember"*, *"we didn't use X"*, *"was handled by others"*, or *"came from the technical architect"*. On the Kafka answer that carries two explicit limitation clauses, the counter returns **0**.

5. **39% of answered production questions volunteer at least one negative that nobody asked for.** 38 unsolicited negatives across 22 of 57 answered questions, in four shapes: recall limits (15), scope limits (11), absences (8), ownership disclaimers (4). The Marsh question is the worst: 3–5 per draw, every draw.

6. **A good answer is being destroyed because of how it opens.** *"Describe a technical disagreement and how you resolved it"* — a standard senior-interview question — was refused on **3 of 3 draws**, at a healthy retrieval score of 0.6525. The model had written a real, substantive, honest answer each time. It was thrown away because its first sentence began "I don't have a specific interpersonal disagreement story…" before going on to give one. The interviewer saw a blank refusal.

7. **The leak scanner still produces false refusals, and the evidence it saves cannot explain them.** *"How does RAG work in this Standing Interview"* was refused on 1 of 3 draws at the **highest retrieval score in the whole bank (0.8306)** because a phrase matched the "the source material" pattern. The review row that was written to diagnose it stores only the first 600 characters — and the offending phrase is not in those 600 characters. The row cannot explain its own block. The ledger holds four `leak_blocked` rows in total; two have this defect and two predate the field entirely.

8. **The recruiter-facing page never says whose interview this is.** A real browser pass found the page says "I", "my own recorded engineering knowledge" and nothing else: the Owner's name appears nowhere. It also has no `aria-live` region, so a screen-reader user gets no announcement when the answer arrives. 21 of 23 browser checks passed; these were the two failures.

9. **The Standing Interview has no browser test and no eval set at all.** It is the only recruiter-facing surface with zero Playwright coverage — JD Match, Ask Codebase, Learn, Usage, Workbench and the customer app all have specs. It is absent from the canonical nav list the nav-consistency spec enforces. There is no labelled answer key anywhere, so answer *quality* has never been measured by anything except the Owner reading the live page.

10. **The public endpoint is unauthenticated, uncapped, and its successful answers are unattributable.** Anyone can post questions to `/api/standing-interview/ask` with no cooldown, no per-IP limit and no daily cap — JD Match has all three; this does not. Successful answers write no `standing_interview_*` row at all, so there is no record of what the system told anybody. Token counts *are* captured — `reasoning_gateway` bridges them into the ledger as generic `model_usage` rows — but those rows carry no `run_id`, no `source`, no `cost_usd`, no `duration_ms`, and a hardcoded `environment='local'` even for Railway calls, so nothing ties them to this surface except a timestamp. *(This point was corrected during independent re-verification: the audit's first draft said the cost was recorded nowhere, which was wrong.)*

## What is actually in front of the model

`VERIFIED_IN_CODE` (`standing_interview.py:934-957`) and `VERIFIED_BY_RUN`. The context is, in order:

1. The frozen system prompt (~140 lines).
2. `WHO YOU ARE` + the 5,878-character career map, introduced as *"It is true and you may draw on it freely."*
3. Six excerpts, each labelled with its employer.
4. The attribution policy line, when one exists.
5. The question.

**Are "did-not-do" facts included, and how are they framed?** Yes — in two places, and in both they are framed as **content to speak from, not as a constraint on speech**:

- The attribution policy line names them explicitly. Real composed line for *"explain your experience with Kafka"* (`VERIFIED_BY_RUN` against the real corpus):
  > `Your own record says: hands-on at BCBSA; not used at NRG Energy. Lead with BCBSA and give it the answer. Do not give a paragraph to any employer marked not used, and do not mention an employer that is not named here; an absence is at most one closing sentence.`
  The final clause is a **budget for an absence, not a prohibition on one**.
- The excerpts themselves. The books record absences in audit vocabulary, and the prompt's response is to *translate* rather than *suppress*: `standing_interview.py:244-249` tells the model to render "no genuine evidence of X" as *"we didn't use X there"*. The whole apparatus assumes the absence will be spoken and only argues about its wording.

There is no mechanism anywhere — prompt, policy line or gate — that says *do not raise an absence the interviewer did not ask about*.

## The two prompt sections that produce the Owner's complaint

Quoted verbatim from the public repository (`agent/standing_interview.py`):

`:229-235`
```
WHAT YOU DID, AND WHERE THAT STOPPED
State the hands-on work plainly, then the boundary of it in one sentence --
what you wrote, versus what someone else ran. Then stop. At most ONE short
sentence anywhere in the answer about something you cannot recall; pick the
single thing an interviewer is most likely to ask next (exact names, exact
settings) and leave it at that.
```

`:291-294`
```
NEVER CLAIM TO HAVE OWNED THE ARCHITECTURE. You implemented within designs
that a lead or architect owned, and you say so in one clause when it matters.
```

The first makes the boundary sentence **mandatory** — "State … then the boundary of it" — on every answer, not permitted on some.

The second is weaker than this audit's first draft claimed, and the independent re-verification was right to say so: it is a **prohibition** plus a *conditional* instruction ("when it matters") to state the positive form — "I implemented within designs a lead owned". It licenses the disclaimer; it does not demand it. The conclusion stands, but it rests on `:229-235` and on two further sections that supply absence content outright — `:205-208` ("say where else it did or did not come up") and `:217-219` ("An absence gets one closing sentence") — not on `:291-294`.

The three sentences the Owner objected to in the Marsh answer are one boundary sentence, one licensed ownership disclaimer and one recall limit. **DESIGN/SPEC failure, not MODEL failure.**

## The Owner's two examples: confirmed root causes

**Example 1 — Kafka.** `VERIFIED_LIVE`, reproduced verbatim in draw 2:
> "I didn't administer the Kafka cluster itself, so I don't have the topic names, partition counts, consumer-group design, or the exact retry and dead-letter configuration for consumer failures…"
> "We didn't use Kafka at NRG — that async work ran on SQS instead."

Root causes, in order of contribution:
1. `standing_interview.py:229-235` — the prompt **requires** a boundary sentence and tells the model to choose "exact names, exact settings". `VERIFIED_IN_CODE`.
2. `standing_interview.py:693-718` — `attribution_line()` injects "not used at NRG Energy" and allocates it "at most one closing sentence". `VERIFIED_BY_RUN` (the composed line is quoted in Part 1).
3. `standing_interview.py:821-827` — `_LIMIT_MARKER` does not match "I don't remember", so the cap never engages. **Measured: draw 1 carries four limiting sentences and the counter returns 0.** `VERIFIED_BY_RUN`. There is a **second defect in the same counter**, surfaced by the independent re-verification: `:893` counts *sentences*, not clauses, so the two limitation clauses in draw 2 sit in one sentence and would score 1 — still under the cap of 2 — even if the marker list were perfect. Fixing the vocabulary alone would not fix the gate.

The Owner's hypothesis — *"the did-not-do boundary facts reach the model as speakable content rather than as constraints"* — is **confirmed for cause 2**. It is **not the whole story**: cause 1 is bigger, and it operates with no attribution data at all.

**Example 2 — Marsh.** `VERIFIED_LIVE`, draw 1:
> "Architecture decisions on the platform came from the technical architect; I implemented within that design rather than owning it."
> "…pipeline operation itself — Jenkins, Nexus — was someone else's responsibility, not mine."
> "I don't have a specific Marsh production incident I can walk through with confidence…"

Root cause: **`attribution_for()` returns `{}` for this question** — "explain your project at marsh in detail" matches no rewrite family, so there is no policy line at all. `VERIFIED_BY_RUN`. The three disclaimers come purely from `:291-294` (ownership) and `:229-235` (boundary + recall limit). This example **disproves** the theory that the attribution data is the cause: here there is none, and the behaviour is worse.

Of the three sentences, `_LIMIT_MARKER` recognises **one**. The gate allows two. It passes.

## (e) The phrase ban-list, and why it cannot converge

How it was built (`VERIFIED_IN_CODE`, from the comments at `:734-810`): by reading real output and adding the phrase that appeared. The comments record the sequence honestly — `"genuine"` banned → the model produced `"no real evidence"` → the adjective alternation widened → a live answer produced `"a single SQS mention showed up in some filing material"` → three more patterns → the independent QA pass found `"a confirmed real async flow"` and `"never came up anywhere in that codebase"` on a fresh draw → two more patterns. `:766-767` states the conclusion in the file itself: *"a banned-phrase list is never finished by reasoning about it; it is finished by reading real output."*

Why it structurally cannot converge:
1. **The target is a meaning; the list matches strings.** "Audit register" is a register of *register*, not of vocabulary. Natural language has unbounded paraphrase, so each ban removes one realisation of an infinite set.
2. **The retry makes it worse in a specific way.** The retry names the matched phrase and the whole ban list (`:1078-1090`) — so a violating draw is pushed toward the *nearest unbanned* paraphrase. The mechanism actively searches the space the list does not cover. The file records this happening: *"A previous retry removed the phrase it was told about and immediately used a different one from this list."*
3. **Growth has a real cost.** Each pattern is a new false-positive surface. This audit measured two live false refusals (questions j and m), and the file documents three earlier ones (`source service`, `substring`, the `rag` provenance case). The list is now 30 patterns; its false-positive rate is now a product risk comparable to its true-positive value.
4. **It cannot express the actual rule.** The Owner's rule is *"state what I did; mention limits only when asked."* That is a statement about **what was asked** and **what was volunteered** — a relation between question and answer. No phrase list can encode a relation.

The fix is not a better list. It is a different oracle: compare the answer against what the question asked for.

## (f) Fail-first and seeded-mutation evidence

**Fail-first.** The Sprint 13 close-out reports that each new test was observed failing before its fix, and the practice is real — `si_acceptance.py:361-372` even records why the Sprint 12 checks could *not* be proved red against the old module and pins a verbatim Owner-rejected answer instead, which is exactly the right response. But the evidence lives in prose and commit messages. **There is no machine-readable record of which tests were observed failing first**, so it cannot be re-checked at `HEAD` by anything. Status: **`UNVERIFIED` as a property of the suite** — not disputed, not provable now. (This is why APPENDIX B item BK-21 exists.)

**Seeded mutations.** Sprint 13 seeded three mutations for the durable workflow and three for JD Match, each caught. `VERIFIED` as reported for those modules.

**Does that prove specification correctness?** **No — and this audit can demonstrate the difference rather than assert it.** A seeded mutation flips a line of code and asks whether any test notices. That measures *code-change sensitivity*: it proves the test is wired to the code path. It says nothing about whether the specification the code implements is the right one. The proof is in this report: the SI code is correct against its specification — the boundary sentence is produced because `:229-235` demands it, the absence is spoken because `attribution_line()` budgets for it — and every gate agrees, and the Owner rejects the output. A mutation test would have caught someone *deleting* the boundary-sentence instruction. It could never have caught the instruction being **wrong**.

The missing complement is a **semantic mutation**: leave the code intact, feed in an output that follows every stated rule and is still a bad interview answer, and require the suite to fail. Today it would pass — measured, six times, in Part 2.

## (g) A target testing strategy (described, not implemented)

1. **An Owner-labelled answer key.** For each question, the Owner fills three fields: *must mention*, *must not volunteer*, *must refuse (Y/N)*. 3. **Over-refusal checks.** A `must_answer` list of legitimate interview questions that must **never** come back refused — behavioural questions included. Question m would fail this today, and should.
4. **Tense checks.** Assert present tense for NRG in any current-work question and past tense for BCBSA and Marsh. Cheap, deterministic, currently absent, currently passing anyway.

6. **Stability over N ≥ 5 draws.** Report `pass^k`, not `pass`. Question j's 1-in-3 refusal and question m's 3-in-3 would both have surfaced before a human noticed. (BK-35.)

# APPENDIX C — the unsolicited-negative detector

This is the audit's own oracle, not shipped code. It classifies a sentence as a negative in one of four categories and counts it as *unsolicited* unless the question itself asked about a limit. It was run over all 63 archived production answers; its output is the `U-neg` column in Appendix A and the taxonomy table in Part 2. It is offered as the starting point for SI-04 and SI-05, not as a finished gate — it has not been tuned against an Owner-labelled key, because none exists yet.

```python
# THE AUDIT'S OWN ORACLE (not shipped code).
# Four categories of negative. A sentence matching any pattern is a negative;
NEG = [
 ("ABSENCE", re.compile(r"\b(did ?n[o']t|didn.t|never|no|wasn.t|weren.t|was not|were not)\s+"
                        r"(\w+\s+){0,3}(use|used|using|part of|in the stack|involved|come up|there)\b", re.I)),
 ("ABSENCE", re.compile(r"\b(wasn.t|was not|isn.t|is not|weren.t)\s+(\w+\s+){0,3}(part of|in|on)\s+(the|that|my|our)\s+(stack|system|platform|estate|project)\b", re.I)),
 ("ABSENCE", re.compile(r"\bno\s+\w+\s+(at|in|on)\s+(NRG|BCBSA|Marsh)\b", re.I)),
 ("SCOPE_LIMIT", re.compile(r"\bI\s+(did ?n[o']t|didn.t|was ?n[o']t|wasn.t)\s+(\w+\s+){0,3}"
                            r"(administer|set up|own|run|operate|configure|manage|design|handle|do)\b", re.I)),
 ("SCOPE_LIMIT", re.compile(r"\b(outside|beyond)\s+(what|the scope of what)\s+I\s+(touched|did|owned|worked)", re.I)),
 ("SCOPE_LIMIT", re.compile(r"\b(someone else.s responsibility|handled by others|was not mine|wasn.t mine|not my (job|responsibility)|that side was outside)\b", re.I)),

                             r"(\w+\s+){0,3}(have|remember|recall|retain|speak to|tell you|say)\b", re.I)),
 ("RECALL_LIMIT", re.compile(r"\bI\s+don.t\s+have\b", re.I)),
 ("OWNERSHIP_DISCLAIMER", re.compile(r"\b(came from the (technical )?architect|rather than owning it|"
                                     r"implementing within that design|implemented within that design|"
                                     r"I was implementing within|within a design (that )?(a|the) (lead|architect))\b", re.I)),
]
# questions where stating a limit IS the answer the interviewer asked for
LIMIT_SOLICITED = {"n", "o", "v"}
# A negative is UNSOLICITED unless the question itself asked about a limit.
```

**Known limits of this detector, stated rather than hidden:** it is regex-based and therefore has the same convergence problem Part 4(e) describes for the shipped ban list — the difference is that it classifies a *relation* (was this asked for?) rather than a vocabulary, so its categories are stable even when its patterns are incomplete. It was validated in one direction only: all 45 raw hits were inspected individually, and **7 were its own false positives** (6 matched the product's standard refusal line on rows that were never answered; 1 matched question r's correct injection refusal), leaving 38 genuine ones. Two obvious hardenings follow from that inspection and are **not** yet applied here: skip any response whose `outcome` is not `answered`, and exclude the product's own refusal string. It was **not** measured for false negatives against a labelled key, so its recall is `UNVERIFIED`.

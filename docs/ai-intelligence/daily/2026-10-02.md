## 8. One Experiment Worth Running

**A concrete, personal way to check whether a "turn off this automated model-switching behavior" setting in a coding assistant actually works: turn the setting on, then deliberately run ordinary (non-malicious) work that's historically tripped the tool's safety filter, and check afterward whether the tool quietly answered with a different, weaker model than the one you asked for — without ever telling you it had switched.**
This turns one person's bug report into your own verified answer about whether a setting you might be relying on actually does what its name says in your own setup, rather than assuming it does.

## 12. Durable-Knowledge Candidates

**"A safety-override setting can be silently defeated by the very subsystem it's supposed to control" is worth remembering as its own distinct failure category** — separate from "the filter is too sensitive" and separate from "a safeguard has an untested code path." This is specifically about the gap between a control existing and being documented, versus the control's actual, verified effect under the exact condition it claims to handle.

**"Usage-volume metrics" and "outcome/efficiency metrics" are different kinds of business evidence, and it's worth noticing which one you're being shown.** A disclosure that says "X million people/searches/transactions used this" tells you adoption is happening; a disclosure that says "this made something measurably faster, cheaper, or more accurate than before" tells you it's actually working better. Both are real and useful, but they answer different questions.

**"A single aggregate benchmark ranking can coexist with the same model trailing specific named competitors on specific named sub-benchmarks" is a durable method for evaluating any big benchmark claim**: the right response to a headline "#1" claim is always "on which specific test, and is there independent corroboration," not acceptance of the headline rank alone.

**"A quantified, repeatable before/after test, even from a single source, is stronger evidence than an anecdotal complaint" is a durable template for evaluating any model-upgrade claim** — a specific test with a clear comparison (e.g., a measured failure-rate difference between two model versions on the same task) is far more useful and checkable than a vague "feels worse" reaction, even when both come from just one person.
